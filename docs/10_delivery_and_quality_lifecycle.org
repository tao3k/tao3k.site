* Purpose

Deployment is the complete operational scope in which a system exists: machines,
environments, topology, configuration, access boundaries, runtime observation,
and lifecycle ownership.  It is not merely the final release step and it is not
synonymous with a Delivery plan.

A Delivery plan is one auditable change plan within a Deployment.  It accepts a
qualified result from the same lifecycle that creates knowledge, specifications,
software, and evidence.  This gives local, on-premises, hybrid, and cloud
deployments one accountable delivery standard.

* Planes and scope

#+begin_example
Control Plane
├── declared intent, authority, and desired state
├── Org Contract and accepted source references
├── SDD: specification-driven development
├── BDD: behaviour and acceptance scenarios
├── tests, scenario benchmarks, and qualification
└── Delivery plans for individual proposed changes
        ↓ qualified artifact and approved change
Delivery Plane
├── reproducible build and package
├── artifact promotion and release orchestration
├── atomic rollout, verification, and rollback
└── delivery receipts
        ↓ operated in
Deployment scope
├── target machines and environments
├── declared topology, configuration, and access boundaries
└── operational state, runtime receipts, and lifecycle ownership
#+end_example

The Control Plane determines whether a change is qualified to proceed.  The
Delivery Plane carries that qualified change into operation and can reverse it
atomically.  A Deployment is the concrete scope in which the system runs:
machines, environments, topology, configuration, and ongoing responsibility.

A Delivery plan belongs to the Control Plane.  It answers /what change is being
made/, /which requirements and quality gates apply/, and /which Deployment scope
may receive it/.  It is not a synonym for the Delivery Plane.

* The lifecycle

#+begin_example
Human intent and source material
        ↓
Org record and Contract
        ↓
Specification-driven development (SDD)
        ↓
Implementation, tests, and scenario benchmarks
        ↓
Qualified build artifact and deployment declaration
        ↓
Atomic deployment or rollback
        ↓
Runtime receipt, review, and durable evidence
        ↺
Improved knowledge and high-quality future training data
#+end_example

** Org record and Contract

Org is the durable human-readable source of intent, provenance, references,
assumptions, and acceptance conditions.  An Org Contract establishes the
required shape of a record before an Agent can treat it as usable input.  The
contract is therefore a data-quality boundary, not a decorative document
template.

** Specification-driven development

SDD turns accepted intent into explicit, reviewable requirements before
implementation.  An Agent may help draft, connect, and check the specification,
but cannot erase the named human owner, the source references, or the acceptance
conditions.  Changes remain linked to the evidence that motivated them.

** Build and qualification

Implementation is built and tested under the declared project rules.  Scenario
benchmarks measure claims such as ingestion throughput, retrieval quality,
resource coverage, latency, or reproducibility before those claims appear in a
customer-facing product page.  A build result is eligible for delivery only when
the declared checks and evidence are present.

** Delivery Plane, Deployment, and runtime evidence

Nix, Bazel, and HCL support the Delivery Plane: reproducible builds, declared
artifacts, controlled promotion, atomic rollout, verification, and rollback.
The Delivery Plane does not decide whether a change is admissible; it carries a
qualified change issued by the Control Plane.

A Deployment may describe local machines, on-premises infrastructure, a hybrid
estate, or cloud environments.  Each Deployment scope has explicit topology,
configuration, access boundaries, operational state, and lifecycle ownership.
Delivery receipts and runtime receipts return to the Org evidence layer for
review and future improvement.

* Quality data for Agent work and future training

The lifecycle produces high-quality information only when every stage retains:

- provenance :: where a source, assertion, result, or decision came from.
- structure :: contracts, schemas, stable references, and explicit semantics.
- qualification :: tests, benchmarks, review, and declared acceptance criteria.
- accountability :: a named human authority for consequential decisions.
- feedback :: runtime receipts and corrections that refine the next iteration.

This prevents a system from treating generated text, disconnected test output,
or an unreviewed deployment as equivalent to evidence.  The goal is not merely
to consume AI training data; it is to produce trustworthy, reusable data about
how knowledge became qualified action.

* Website information architecture

The public site should explain this lifecycle as a linked system rather than
repeat its prose on every page.

- Platform / Control Plane :: describes declared authority, Org Contracts, SDD,
  BDD, qualification, benchmarks, and change-specific Delivery plans.
- Platform / Delivery Plane :: describes Nix, Bazel, HCL, artifact promotion,
  atomic rollout, rollback, and delivery receipts.  It targets explicit
  Deployment scopes: environments, topology, machines, and operational
  boundaries.
- Products :: describe how a concrete system consumes and produces qualified
  resources.  [[https://github.com/tao3k/xiuxian-artisan-workshop][tao3k/xiuxian-artisan-workshop]]
  is one such system, not a substitute name for the complete lifecycle.
- Solutions :: show a customer outcome and reference the exact Product,
  Platform capability, and deployment model involved.

Org-WASM resolves these references into the site so that a product claim can
lead to its deployment requirement, benchmark evidence, and source record
without copying each statement into separate marketing pages.
